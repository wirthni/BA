\section{ALICE at the Large Hadron Collider}

\subsection{The Large Hadron Collider}

The Large Hadron Collider is a hadron collider in a 26.7km underground tunnel at the CERN particle research facility. \cite{evans_lhc_2008} Its original design concept foresaw the acceleration of particles to a center-of-mass energy of up to $14$TeV in the two rings with counter-rotating beams. The aim of such high particle energies is to reveal physics beyond the Standard Model. The LHC is capable of colliding heavy ions, like stripped lead ions ($^{208}\text{Pb}^{82+}$), with a per-nucleon energy of $2.76$TeV which are principally provided at Interaction Point 2 for the specialized ALICE detector.

\subsection{ALICE - A Large Ion Collider Experiment}

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{Media/Pictures/ALICE_CAD_Overview.png}
	\caption{The ALICE experiment at CERN, LHC. The central-barrel detectors (ITS, TPC, TRD, TOF, PHOS, EMCal, and HMPID) are embedded in a solenoid with magnetic field B = 0.5 T. Image taken from \cite{collaboration_performance_2014}}
	\label{fig:ALICE_CAD_Overview}
\end{figure}

ALICE (A Large Ion Collider Experiment) is one of the biggest experiments at the Large Hadron Collider. \cite{collaboration_performance_2014} Its main goal is to study quantum chromodynamics and in particular a state of deconfined quarks and gluons at very high energy densities. This state of matter was also present in the early universe, very shortly after the big bang. To achieve such high energy densities, nuclei are accelerated to extremely high energies in the LHC and brought to collision inside of ALICE. Possible collisions may involve heavy-ion nuclei (AA), only protons (pp) or protons and nuclei (pA). The latter are regarded as control measurements, because they are needed to separate the QCD-matter signals from other possible effects.

The ALICE detector itself has a diameter of 16m, a length of 26m and weighs about 10.000 tons. \cite{collaboration_performance_2014} It glances with a high spatial resolution and a low transverse momentum threshold of $p_T^{min} \approx 0.15 \unit{GeV/c}$, meaning that low-momentum particles produced in the collision, can be detected reliably. Furthermore, it serves a good particle identification for particles up to $20 \unit{GeV/c}$.

ALICE is composed of seventeen detector systems (see figure \ref{fig:ALICE_CAD_Overview}). \cite{collaboration_performance_2014} The central-barrel detectors, specifically the Inner Tracking System (ITS), the Time Projection Chamber (TPC), the Transition Radiation Detector (TRD), the Time Of Flight Detector (TOF), the Photon Spectrometer (PHOS), the Electromagnetic Calorimeter (EMCal) and the High Momentum Particle Identification Detector (HMPID), are embedded a solenoid magnet of $B = 0.5\unit{T}$. The typical central acceptance of these detectors is $\abs{\eta} \leq 0.9$ (see chapter \ref{sec:AnalysisOfHeavyIonCollisionData}).
