\section{Analysis of heavy ion collision data}

When analyzing heavy ion collision data from ALICE, one has to know first which quantities, may they be event-related or particle-, so track-related, are important to be taken care of for each specific analysis. For serving the purpose of this thesis, we will therefore only take into account those quantities that are or could be needed to conduct an heavy ion analysis regarding the diffusion wake. Starting at where all particles come from, let us first look at interesting event, so collision, properties. In the first chapter we have talked about the phenomenologies of when two heavy ion nuclei collide at collider energies in the case where they fully overlap. What has not been talked about yet is what happens when two nuclei collide only peripherally, or non-centrally. The centrality of the collision of two nuclei is characterized by the impact parameter $b$ between the nuclei, so the distance between their centers in the transverse direction. \cite{collaboration_centrality_2021} The smaller the impact parameter is, the bigger the overlap region of the nuclei is, thus increasing the size and shape of the resulting medium. The outcome of the collision directly depends on the number of nucleons that participate, but this quantity is not directly accessible and hence need to be derived from other quantities that scale approximately with the number of participating nucleons, such as the particle multiplicity. The centrality of events is categorized from 0-100\%, where low values describe the most central events, e.g. 0-10\%. Another effect that is needed to be understood for this analysis and is also related to event properties is elliptic flow. When nuclei collide non-centrally, the overlap region is shaped like a lentil, shown in figure \ref{fig:ellipticflow}.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{Media/Pictures/EllipticFlow}
	\caption{Schematic illustrations of a $\sqrt{S_{NN}} = 200\unit{GeV}$ Au+Au collision with a 6 fm impact parameter. The left panel shows the nucleons of the two colliding nuclei with an ellipse outlining the approximate interaction region. The right panel shows a momentum-space representation of $v_2$. The average radius of each successive ring represents the $p_T$ of the particles while the anisotropy of the ring represents the magnitude of $v_2$. The highest $p_T$ particles (outer-ring) exhibit the strongest v2 while the lowest $p_T$ particles (inner-ring) exhibit a vanishingly small $v_2$. From \cite{collaboration_centrality_2021}}
	\label{fig:ellipticflow}
\end{figure}

Given this initial lentil shape of the overlap region, it is interesting to look at whether this shape also shows up in the distribution of particles in the momentum space.  \cite{collaboration_centrality_2021} A Fourier decomposition is used to describe the azimuthal dependence of the final momentum-space distributions: \cite{voloshin_flow_1996}

\begin{equation}
	\frac{d^3 N}{p_T d p_T dy d(\phi - \psi)} = \frac{1}{2 \pi} \frac{d N}{p_T d p_T dy} \cdot \left[1 + 2 v_2 \cos(\phi - \psi) + 2 v_2 \cos(2 (\phi - \psi)) + \dots \right]
\end{equation}

where $\phi$ is the azimuth angle of the particle, $y$ is the longitudinal rapidity, $p_T$ the transverse momentum an $\psi$ is the reaction plane angle defined by the vector connecting the centers of the two colliding nuclei That is the $x$-axis in the case of figure \ref{fig:ellipticflow}. $v_2 > 0$ describes the case where more particles are emitted along the short axis of the overlap region. The bigger $v_2$, the stronger the particle correlation asymmetry is.  It comes out that $v_2 > 0$ for typical per-nucleon energies at the LHC, \cite{collaboration_centrality_2021} so we will expect an enhancement of particles in the direction of the reaction plane. Because we will be investigating particle correlation differences at order of magnitudes smaller than the effect of the elliptic flow, $\psi$ will be an event property to keep in mind.
