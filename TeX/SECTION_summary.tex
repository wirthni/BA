\section{Summary and Outlook}

\subsection{ALICE RUN 3 measurements}

The jet-induced diffusion wake was successfully measured using the correlation of soft hadrons with a dijet/dihadron trigger pair in ALICE RUN 3 0-10\% Pb--Pb data at $\sqrt{s_{NN}} = 5.36\bunit{TeV}$. No compensation for detector acceptance or tracking efficiency effects was made.

The analysis results using a correlated dijet pair as trigger revealed anomalies as systematic errors at $|\eta| = 0.3$, especially for lower jet trigger $p_T$ thresholds. Apart from that the diffusion wake signal could be identified among all trigger variants, \newline $(40,20)\bunit{GeV/c}, (30,15)\bunit{GeV/c}, (20,10)\bunit{GeV/c}$.

The result of the $(40,20)\bunit{GeV/c}$ trigger variant was compared to another ALICE RUN 3 analysis that uses 0-30\% Pb--Pb data. This 0-30\% data includes one Pb--Pb dataset that is also used in the 0-10\% analysis. The 0-10\% result is very similar to the 0-30\% result although the 0-10\% result is subject to a small systematic error. Only the 0-30\% signal is asymmetric in pseudorapidity as expected. It also shows a smaller residual signal for higher soft hadron bins as expected. The 0-10\% result for the $(40,20)\bunit{GeV/c}$ trigger variant was also compared to a CoLBT-hydro prediction \cite{zhong_yang_colbt-hydro_2025}. The prediction provides data for the soft hadron bins $1 \leq p_T \leq 2 \bunit{GeV/c}$ and $2 \leq p_T \leq 4 \bunit{GeV/c}$. The $1 \leq p_T \leq 2 \bunit{GeV/c}$ prediction fits the 0-10\% result well for $\eta > 0$, but not for $\eta < 0$. There, the systematic error shifts the diffusion wake signal out of the prediction's bounds. The $2 \leq p_T \leq 4 \bunit{GeV/c}$ prediction fits the ALICE 0-10\% data well.

The results of the diffusion wake analysis using a correlated dihadron pair as trigger is not subject to anomalies and reveals less noisy results across all trigger variants, $(30,15)\bunit{GeV/c}, (20,15)\bunit{GeV/c}, (15,10)\bunit{GeV/c}, (15,8)\bunit{GeV/c}$. The $(15,8)\bunit{GeV/c}$ trigger variant was added afterwards because the $(15,10)\bunit{GeV/c}$ variant showed promising results. The diffusion wake signal is clearly visible in many $p_T$ bins. The $(15,8)\bunit{GeV/c}$ trigger variant for soft hadrons with transverse momenta $0 \leq p_T \leq 2 \bunit{GeV/c}$ holds the clearest results in terms of uncertainties and symmetry of the diffusion wake signal.

To give quantitatively relevant results on the diffusion wake measurement it is a part of the next iteration to account for detector acceptance effects and tracking efficiency effects.

\subsection{Simulated Pb--Pb event measurements}

The jet-induced diffusion wake was also studied using the correlation of soft hadrons with a dijet/dihadron trigger pair in artificial Pb--Pb data derived from real Pb--Pb data and the PYTHIA event generator.

The diffusion wake analysis showed a difference signal that opposes the predicted diffusion wake signal by CoLBT-hydro \cite{zhong_yang_colbt-hydro_2025}. The opposing signal is smaller than the diffusion wake signal, but of same shape albeit inverted in $\eta$. It is stronger when using a dijet trigger compared to using a dihadron trigger. The bias increases with decreasing dijet/dihadron trigger pair $p_T$ threshold. 

The bias signal was then further studied by magnifying the simulated directed flow low-$p_T$ hadrons are subject to. The, by a factor of 100, increased directed flow revealed a difference signal that has similar characteristics like the CoLBT-hydro diffusion wake signal \cite{zhong_yang_colbt-hydro_2025}. A mechanism that explains the dependence of the signal on the strength of the directed flow was introduced. It needs further study and more parameter manipulation to determine which parameter change could generate the predicted diffusion wake magnitudes. Nevertheless this analysis has shown that not only the diffusion wake could generate a signal of such kind. It is therefore of great importance to implement the strength of flow effects for a variety of kinematics for one specific collision energy $\sqrt{s_{NN}}$ and centrality category. To be precise, the flow parameters $v_n$ depending on pseudorapidity $\eta$ and transverse momentum $p_T$ must be known in a big range, e. g. $0 \leq p_T \leq 40 \bunit{GeV}$ to make quantitatively reliable statements on such biases. In this early stage of the simulation, a broad variety of flow coefficient measurements has been used. This includes several centrality categories, different center of mass energies and small $p_T$ windows in which the measurements are done.

\subsection{Azimuthal shift of reconstructed jets}

Using the Pb--Pb event simulation it was studied where a jet is clustered by the anti-$k_t$ algorithm with and without underlying soft hadron background that is subject to elliptic flow. The results are presented separately for different jet transverse momentum bins. The result shows that a jet's azimuthal position is biased towards the event plane. The effect is strongest for the softest jets. For jets with transverse momenta of $20 \leq p_T \leq 40 \bunit{GeV/c}$ the shift is highest when the jet is approximately $d = \pm \pi/4 \bunit{rad}$ away from the event plane. There the jet is shifted by $0.836 \bunit{deg}$ towards the event plane. For higher jet $p_T$ the value decreases rapidly. The introduced low-particle model of the cluster algorithm predicts the most shift when $d = \pm \pi/4 \bunit{rad}$ exactly, in the measurement the maximum is slightly off. The model uses a few approximations whose validity may need further attention in order to solve the deviation of the model from the measurement.
